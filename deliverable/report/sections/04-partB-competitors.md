# Part B — Competitors

## B.1 — The competitive map

**How I split direct vs. indirect:** *direct* = solves the same job (get better at using AI for work) for the same audience (non-technical professionals) in a substitutable format; *indirect* = competes for the same attention, budget, or acquisition channel while differing in subject or format. Substitutes (free tools) are a third, and — as the research shows — the *most dangerous* category.

| Competitor | Type | Why it competes |
|---|---|---|
| **Coursiv** | **Direct** — structural twin | Same quiz→app→subscription funnel, same "learn AI for your job," same price band. The closest rival. |
| **Iro AI** | **Direct** — product twin | Literal "Duolingo for AI." Closest *product*, but weak commercially (soft funnel, ~$60/yr, small brand). |
| **Section (SectionAI)** | **Direct** — premium incumbent | Same audience (AI for knowledge workers); credibility via Scott Galloway; now pivoting to an AI-coach SaaS. |
| **Outskill** | **Direct** — high-ticket funnel | AI upskilling via free workshop → $1–3k cohort; bids in the same ad auctions. |

## B.2 — Teardown highlights (what each does well, and the one thing worth taking)

**Structural twins & ad-funnels.** *Coursiv* runs the identical machine and has branded its retention arc as a **finite "28-Day AI Challenge" with a completion certificate** — turning vague AI dread into a *closable* loop with a natural renewal moment; its **275 BBB complaints over undisclosed recurring pricing** are the avoidable failure mode. *Iro AI* ships a "Prompt Lab" (graded practice against a live model) that kills the "too passive/basic" complaint. *Outskill* proves the *same fearful buyer will pay $500–3,000* for a live time-boxed event. *Be10x* uses a **~$1 commitment tripwire** to manufacture sunk-cost show-up commitment from low-intent ad traffic.

**Premium & media incumbents.** *Section* has pivoted from courses to an AI-coach SaaS — the loudest signal in the field: even the premium incumbent admits courses alone don't retain, so it's bolting on a recurring *tool* with saveable, reusable use-cases. *The Rundown* grows a 2M+ newsletter via **beehiiv Boosts at ~$2/subscriber** (moved ~50% of budget off Meta) and prices University at the **"expense-it" $1,008** threshold — **~70% bill it to employer L&D**. *Mimo* is the UX playbook: a ~15-step commitment-escalating onboarding and the **"Blinkist paywall" experiment (trial-timeline screen + pre-expiry reminder) that doubled trial opt-ins (+100%) and cut 24h cancellations 25%**.

**The template & the substitutes.** *Duolingo* proves the streak is the moat (7-day streak = 2.4–3.6× continuation). But the sharpest strategic fact in Part B is that the content itself is free: ChatGPT/Claude, YouTube, and newsletters give away exactly what Jobescape sells. Jobescape cannot win on content — only on scaffolding, feedback, and accountability (the AI tutor as a *practice surface*, the one thing free substitutes structurally can't provide).

> Reconciling this with Part C (worth stating, because it looks like a contradiction). Part B says the streak is Duolingo's moat; Part C recommends *removing* gamification from the Challenge. Both are right, because Duolingo's real lesson is **daily pacing**, not the flame. What retains is that today's lesson is available today and tomorrow's is not — Jobescape's Challenge copied the pageantry (a streak counter, a badge, a certificate) and skipped the pacing, which is why its streak displays 0 and 27% of finishers binge the whole thing in a day. And Duolingo's audience is not Jobescape's: ~60% of Jobescape's buyers are 45+, and the under-25s who respond best to streak mechanics are 9% of the book and churn at 31%. Take the rhythm, leave the confetti.


## B.3 — What to take, in priority order (and why this order)

The order follows the money: de-risk the fragile economics first, then build retention, then defensibility, then diversify acquisition, then expand monetization. (Ties directly to Part D: the value is a fragile recurring tail, so protecting it beats squeezing the funnel.)

1. **Copy Mimo's "Blinkist paywall" verbatim** (trial-timeline + pre-expiry reminder). *Why first:* proven **+100% trial opt-in / −25% early cancels**, near-zero build cost, and it directly attacks the refund/chargeback risk that threatens the recurring tail. Highest ROI, lowest effort.
2. **Adopt Coursiv's branded finite "Challenge" as the retention container — with honest recurring-price disclosure.** *Why second:* it reframes the whole product around the habit loop (pairs with the Part C v2) *and* defuses the FTC/Genesis-style existential risk. Retention + compliance moat in one move.
3. **Build the practice surface: Iro's "Prompt Lab" (graded practice) + Section's role-specific co-pilot with saveable use-cases.** *Why third:* this is the moat against the real threat — *free substitutes*. It converts the existing chatbot from commoditized content delivery into feedback + switching cost. Medium effort, core to defensibility.
4. **Launch an owned newsletter / daily in-app brief as a CAC hedge (à la The Rundown) + frame the annual plan as "expensable."** *Why fourth:* it reduces 100%-paid-ads dependence and improves Part D break-even the moment blended CAC drops — but it compounds slowly, so start early, harvest later.
5. **Test the offer ladder: Be10x's ~$1 commitment tripwire (lift activation) + Outskill's high-ticket live "AI Mastermind" (lift ARPU).** *Why last:* monetization expansions worth testing *after* retention and differentiation are fixed — a lower entry to raise activation, and a higher-LTV offer for paid ads to bid against.
