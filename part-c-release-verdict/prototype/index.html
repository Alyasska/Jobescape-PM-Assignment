The daily gate — the mechanic v1 never shipped

Challenge v1 had no way to hold a day back, so 27% of the people who finished the “7-day” challenge finished it in one sitting and the habit it was built to create never had a chance to form. This prototype makes one day one day. Move through the five screens below; each note on the right ties the screen to the finding that produced it.

        What v1 could not measure

        • Nothing yet — use the prototype.

        Screen 1 · Commit

        There is currently no scheduled reason to come back.

        v1 has no return trigger tied to a time the user chose, so the feature relies on people spontaneously remembering it. Asking for ten minutes at a named hour is the cheapest possible habit loop, and it makes the reminder honest rather than random.

        only 36.8% of starters who did a lesson ever came back for a second challenge day

        Screen 2 · Today — the gate

        One day, and only one day, is available.

        This is the hypothesis v1 never tested. Nothing in the shipped feature holds a day back, so people either binge it or abandon it. The locked card is deliberately the most prominent thing on the screen — the countdown is the product’s promise that tomorrow exists and has something in it.

        27% of finishers completed the whole “7-day” challenge in a single day; 46% within two

        Screen 3 · The win

        45% of the people who press Start complete zero lessons.

        That is the highest-intent moment in the feature, and nearly half of it produces nothing. The fix is to delete every step between pressing start and holding something useful: no blank prompt box, no syntax, three pre-loaded real situations, a result inside ten minutes.

        499 starters completed zero lessons in a two-week window

        Screen 4 · Done

        Half of the people who finish lesson 1 never open lesson 2.

        The day closes on something the user keeps — a saved, reusable result — and names tomorrow as a concrete work task rather than a topic. No confetti and no badge: the buyer here is a professional in their fifties, and the reward that lands is competence, not celebration.

        lesson 1 → 2 survival 50.3% · challenge CSAT 3.25 vs 3.45 elsewhere

        Screen 5 · Tomorrow

        The gate opening is the moment worth testing.

        The one real risk in this design is that a locked day reads as being shut out of something you paid for. This screen is what you put in front of five customers to find out: does the reminder land as helpful, and does the unlock feel like an invitation or a restriction?

        the decision this prototype exists to de-risk
