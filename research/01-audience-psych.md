# Audience Psychology & Jobs-to-Be-Done: Buyers of "Learn AI at Work" Products (Tier-1 Markets)

**Research step 1 (broad net) for the Jobescape PM take-home — Part A input.**
Scope: US & Western Europe consumers who buy self-paced "use AI at work / for your career" subscriptions.
Method: desk research against authoritative surveys (Pew, McKinsey, WEF, Microsoft/LinkedIn, BCG, Gallup, PwC, Randstad, Deloitte, HBS/NBER) + academic literature on automation anxiety and adult learning.

> **How to read confidence flags:** `[FACT]` = directly sourced statistic. `[INFERENCE]` = my synthesis/interpretation, defensible but not directly measured. `[WEAK]` = single-source, secondary, or methodologically soft — treat as directional only.

---

## 0. TL;DR — the buyer in one paragraph

The core buyer is a **college-educated, non-technical white-collar professional, roughly 25–50, in a Tier-1 knowledge-economy role**, who is **more worried than hopeful** about AI at work, feels **personal pressure to keep up** (FOMO more than acute displacement fear), believes **AI skills now gate hiring and promotion**, and gets **little or no AI training from their employer** — so they self-fund it. They buy a *feeling* (relief from "falling behind" + confidence + identity as "someone who's on top of this") at least as much as a skill. `[INFERENCE, well-supported below]`

## 1. Demographics & psychographics of buyers

There is little public data on buyers of *this specific category* of consumer subscription. The best proxy is **who actually adopts and uses AI at work**, since adoption and willingness-to-pay-to-learn track the same demographics. Consistent finding across sources: **AI-at-work skews younger, more educated, higher-income, and (still, narrowing) more male.**

**Age (US, ChatGPT adoption, Pew, June 2025):** `[FACT]`
- Under 30: **58%** have used ChatGPT · 30–49: **41%** · 50–64: **25%** · 65+: **10%**.
- *For work specifically* (employed adults): 18–29: **38%** · 30–49: **30%** · 50+: **18%**.
- Nearly **half of all ChatGPT messages** are sent by users **under 26** (OpenAI/NBER usage paper).

**Education (US, ChatGPT adoption, Pew, June 2025):** `[FACT]`
- Postgrad: **52%** · Bachelor's: **51%** · Some college: **33%** · High school or less: **18%**.
- Work use rises with education: postgrad **45%**, bachelor's **36%**, some college **25%**, HS or less **17%**.

**Income / occupation:** `[FACT]` "Educated users and users in highly-paid professional occupations are substantially more likely to use ChatGPT for work"; those with **incomes above ~$70K** over-index (OpenAI/NBER, *How People Use ChatGPT*). Among workers in AI-**exposed** occupations, **41%** have used ChatGPT for work, ranging from **65% (marketing)** down to **12% (financial advisors)** — i.e., adoption is occupation-shaped, not just age-shaped.

**Gender:** `[FACT, narrowing]` Global adoption ~**47.8% men vs 39.3% women** (~16–22% relative gap; HBS working paper, n≈318,924). The single biggest explanatory factor is **self-assessed knowledge/confidence**, not access. Women disproportionately *want training* before adopting (Fed NY). Deloitte projected US women's usage to **equal or exceed** men's by end-2025. The gender gap is a **confidence gap**, which is directly monetizable by a "learn AI" product. `[INFERENCE]`

**Psychographic profile (synthesized):** `[INFERENCE]`
- **Anxious but agentic** — worried enough to feel pain, proactive enough to act (buy a course) rather than freeze.
- **Status-conscious knowledge worker** — identity tied to being competent/relevant; AI threatens that self-concept.
- **Time-poor** — treats learning as a means to an end (career/income), not a hobby → needs fast, applicable, bite-sized.
- **Low technical self-efficacy** — "non-technical," intimidated by tools; needs hand-holding and quick wins to sustain motivation.
- **Outcome-oriented / extrinsically primed** — buys for promotion, income, security; intrinsic enjoyment is a *retention* lever, not a *purchase* trigger.

## 2. Jobs-to-be-done & motivations (ranked)

Motivations behind buying career-upskilling (and AI-upskilling specifically). Ranked by strength of purchase-driving evidence in Tier-1 markets:

**Rank 1 — "Don't let me fall behind / stay employable" (fear + status threat).** `[FACT + INFERENCE]`
- **52%** of US workers are **worried** about future AI at work vs **36%** hopeful; **33%** feel **overwhelmed** (Pew, Oct 2024, n=5,273 employed).
- **32%** think AI will mean **fewer** long-run job opportunities for them; only **6%** think more.
- Hiring reality validates the fear: **66%** of leaders **wouldn't hire** someone without AI skills; **71%** would take a *less-experienced* candidate *with* AI skills over a more-experienced one without (Microsoft/LinkedIn WTI 2024).
- This is the **strongest purchase trigger** because it combines a felt threat with a concrete, buyable remedy. It maps 1:1 to Jobescape's ad angles ("humans with AI will replace humans without AI"). `[INFERENCE]`

**Rank 2 — "AI FOMO" / keep up with peers (distinct from displacement fear).** `[FACT + WEAK on exact %]`
- Emerging academic construct: a validated **FOMO-AI scale** with three factors — **backwardness anxiety** (falling behind peers on AI skills), **access concerns** (not enough access to AI info/training), **dividend anxiety** (missing the economic/social upside) (Technology in Society, 2025/26).
- **~74%** of professionals report feeling **anxious or overwhelmed** by AI `[WEAK — secondary aggregation]`.
- FOMO is described as the **dominant** professional response in 2025–26, *replacing* the earlier job-loss-fear wave — it's aspirational-anxiety ("others are pulling ahead"), which is a *better* growth engine than pure dread because it's chronic and peer-driven. `[INFERENCE]`

**Rank 3 — Career advancement & income.** `[FACT]`
- Top reasons adults upskill: **be more effective in the job (60%)**, **learn and grow (51%)**, **fulfill a job requirement (39%)**, **advance/start a new career (20%)**.
- Top *benefits sought*: **career advancement/promotion (40%)**, **higher earning potential (39%)**, staying current with trends (edX/adult-learner surveys).
- **41%** of adult learners pursue further education specifically to **advance their careers**.
- Career-movers are the hottest buyers: workers likely to switch employers in 12 months are **nearly 2× as likely** to strongly weigh upskilling in that decision (**67% vs 36%**, PwC 2024).

**Rank 4 — Productivity / time-saving (relieve overload).** `[FACT]`
- **56%** believe GenAI will **boost their efficiency**; **76%** expect AI to create chances to learn new skills (PwC 2024).
- But workers report **rising workloads (45%)** and accelerating change (**62%** say more change last year than the year before) — so the promise is "AI gives me time back." This maps directly to Jobescape's quiz pain-poke *"used AI then spent just as long fixing it?"* — the JTBD is **"make AI actually save me time instead of creating rework."** `[INFERENCE]`

**Rank 5 — Confidence & identity ("become an AI-capable person").** `[FACT + INFERENCE]`
- Confidence/self-assessed knowledge is the **#1 driver** of the AI gender/adoption gap (HBS) — meaning the felt barrier is psychological, not technical. A course that sells *confidence* addresses the real blocker.
- Andragogy (Knowles): adults' self-concept and identity are central; transformative learning explicitly targets a **growth mindset** and reframing one's self-image. Buying is partly identity-work: "I am the kind of person who's on top of AI." `[INFERENCE]`

**Note on segmentation of motivation:** fear/status threat over-indexes for **mid-career and older** workers in routine/administrative roles; **advancement/income** over-indexes for **ambitious 28–40 climbers**; **FOMO/peer-comparison** over-indexes for **Gen Z / early-career**; **confidence** over-indexes for **women and re-entrants**. (Evidence in §3 and §5.)

## 3. The science & data on AI-at-work anxiety and "AI FOMO"

### 3a. Macro stakes (why the anxiety is rational)
- **WEF Future of Jobs 2025:** AI/automation to **displace ~92M** jobs and **create ~170M** by 2030 (**net +78M**), but **59 of every 100 workers** need reskilling/upskilling by 2030 and **11 are unlikely to get it** (~120M at medium-term redundancy risk). **39%** of core skills will change by 2030. Skills gaps are the **#1 barrier** to transformation (**63%** of employers). **North America** is the highest-training-need region (**67%** of workforce). **85%** of employers prioritize upskilling. `[FACT]`
- **Microsoft/LinkedIn WTI 2024:** **75%** of global knowledge workers already use AI at work (nearly doubled in 6 months). **142×** increase in LinkedIn members adding AI skills; **160%** jump in **non-technical** professionals taking LinkedIn Learning AI courses — direct evidence the *exact target* (non-technical) is self-skilling. Only **39%** have had employer AI training → the training gap is the market gap. `[FACT]`

### 3b. Prevalence of anxiety / displacement fear
- **Pew (Oct 2024):** 52% worried, 36% hopeful, 33% overwhelmed; **lower-income workers least optimistic** (37% expect fewer opportunities, 5% more). `[FACT]`
- **BCG AI at Work 2025:** **72%** use AI regularly (managers 78%, frontline 51%); **41%** globally worry their **role could disappear**; notably, *the more people use AI, the more their concerns grow* — exposure raises, not lowers, anxiety. `[FACT]`
- **Gallup 2025:** **45%** used AI at work at least a few times/year (Q3 2025) — **double** the 21% of Q2 2023; **13%** daily. **18%** think their job likely eliminated by tech in 5 yrs (up from 15%); at AI-adopting companies that rises to **23%**. `[FACT]`
- **McKinsey "Superagency" 2025:** employees are **3× more likely** to be using GenAI than leaders assume; **47%** of employees (vs 20% of C-suite) expect to use AI for ≥30% of work within a year; **48%** say **formal GenAI training** would most boost their usage — demand for structured learning is explicit. `[FACT]`

### 3c. Generational skew (sharp and counter-intuitive)
Younger workers are **more** anxious *and* more proactive — a prime paid-course audience. `[FACT]`
- **52% of Gen Z** (vs 45% millennials, **33% Gen X**) worry about being **replaced by someone more comfortable with AI**.
- **63% of Gen Z** and **65% of millennials** worry GenAI will eliminate jobs (Deloitte).
- Gen Z is the **most proactive** at reskilling: **~19%** already actively learning new skills due to AI concerns, vs 14% millennials, 9% Gen X, ~2% Boomers.
- **Implication:** the highest *anxiety × willingness-to-act* combination sits with **Gen Z / young millennials** — but they also have the least disposable income, a pricing tension. `[INFERENCE]`

### 3d. Academic / psychology literature
- **Automation anxiety → measurable harm:** robot exposure correlates with declines in worker mental well-being via two pathways — **heightened job-loss fear** and a **reduced sense of achievement** (Vienna study, 2024). Automation anxiety is linked to **sleep disturbance** in a nationally representative sample (PMC/Int'l J. Env. Res. Public Health). `[FACT]`
- **Anticipatory/chronic stress:** the threat is *future-oriented*, so it "depletes coping resources before actual displacement occurs" — meaning the pain is present-tense and continuous, which is exactly what makes it monetizable now. `[FACT/INFERENCE]`
- **Who fears most:** anxiety is most pronounced among **older employees and routine-role workers**; **technophobia over-indexes among women, minorities, and less-educated** individuals — tying automation fear to socio-educational inequality. `[FACT]`
- **AI FOMO scale** (backwardness / access / dividend anxiety) — a distinct, peer-comparison construct separate from displacement fear. `[FACT]`
- **Growth mindset & andragogy:** Knowles' 6 principles (need to know, self-concept, prior experience, readiness, problem-centered orientation, intrinsic motivation); transformative-learning theory targets **growth mindset** and reframing. Adults shift from extrinsic toward intrinsic motivation as they progress — supports a "hook on fear/career, retain on mastery/enjoyment" design. `[FACT/INFERENCE]`

## 4. How adults engage with self-paced online learning

**The core problem this category must beat: completion is terrible, and motivation — not difficulty — is the main killer.**

- **Completion rates:** free MOOCs historically **~5–15%** complete; recent/paid figures higher — **15–20% free**, **~55–65% for paid certificates** (Coursera paid ~55%, edX verified ~48%). `[FACT, some 2025 figures are secondary/`[WEAK]`]`
- **Funnel leakage:** **~52%** who register **never even open** the courseware; one famous Coursera course lost **80% before it began**; cumulative dropout can hit **~96% over five years**. `[FACT/WEAK — older, course-specific]`
- **Length is destiny:** completion vs course length — **1–2 hrs: ~80%**, **5 hrs: ~55%**, **10 hrs: ~35%**, **20+ hrs: ~20%**. Short, modular beats comprehensive. `[WEAK — secondary aggregator, but directionally strong]`
- **Why people drop:** **lack of time (38%)**, **lost motivation (25%)**, content too difficult (14%), perceived irrelevance (10%). Two of the top three are motivational/relevance, not cognitive. `[WEAK — secondary]`
- **Paid > free:** payment ("skin in the game") massively lifts completion — supports the paywall-then-app model. `[FACT/INFERENCE]`

**What sustains motivation (the retention levers):**
- **Goal-setting:** learners who set **career goals** engage with learning **4× more** (LinkedIn 2024). → capture a concrete goal in onboarding (the quiz already does this).
- **Relevance / immediate applicability:** **78%** of learners said coursework was **immediately applicable** and 7 in 10 felt more effective at work (Emeritus). Andragogy: design around **tasks learners actually perform** — argues strongly for the **role-specific courses** (Accountants/Lawyers/Sales). `[FACT/INFERENCE]`
- **Habit & gamification (the "Duolingo for AI" thesis):** Duolingo's streak/loss-aversion system drove a **36% YoY DAU** increase (2025); a streak wager lifts **day-14 retention +14%**; "an education app is really a habit app that teaches as a side effect," with **forgiveness mechanisms** (freezes) so one missed day doesn't collapse a streak. Validates streaks, daily micro-lessons, and loss-aversion as retention mechanics for exactly this format. `[FACT/WEAK — vendor/secondary sources]`
- **Bite-sized + quick wins:** short lessons align with both the completion-by-length curve and low technical self-efficacy (need early success to build confidence). `[INFERENCE]`

**Design implication for Jobescape:** the ad→quiz→paywall funnel sells on **fear/career JTBD**; the *product* must switch to **intrinsic + habit + relevance** levers fast, or it inherits MOOC-grade churn. The quiz's goal-capture and pain-profiling is a retention asset if it's actually used to personalize the learning path, not just to sell. `[INFERENCE]`

## 5. Emergent candidate segments (4–6)

Derived from the age × motivation × role evidence above. Format: **who / job-to-be-done / dominant motivation / core pain.** These are hypotheses to validate against the quiz data + creatives in Part A. `[INFERENCE, evidence-anchored]`

1. **The Anxious Catch-Up Professional** — *who:* 38–55, non-technical white-collar (admin, ops, HR, finance-adjacent), mid-career. *job:* stay employable and not be the person who "didn't keep up." *motivation:* fear + status threat (§2 R1; §3d older/routine-role skew). *pain:* overwhelmed, doesn't know where to start, low tech confidence.

2. **The Ambitious Climber** — *who:* 28–40, college-educated, career-focused, likely to job-hop. *job:* get promoted / win the raise / stand out as "the AI person" on the team. *motivation:* advancement + income (§2 R3; PwC 67% vs 36% mover stat). *pain:* employer won't train them; wants a visible edge and, ideally, proof/credential.

3. **The Gen Z / Early-Career Striver** — *who:* 22–29, digital-native but economically anxious. *job:* differentiate in a brutal entry-level market; avoid being out-competed by an AI-savvier peer. *motivation:* AI FOMO / peer comparison (§3c: 52% fear replacement, most proactive reskillers). *pain:* high anxiety, thin budget, wants fast credibility and shareable wins.

4. **The Low-Confidence Re-entrant / Switcher (skews female)** — *who:* career changers and workforce re-entrants; over-indexes women. *job:* rebuild professional relevance and confidence. *motivation:* confidence + identity (§1 gender confidence gap; §2 R5). *pain:* low AI self-efficacy, needs hand-holding, quick wins, and a safe "no dumb questions" environment.

5. **The Burned, Time-Poor Doer** — *who:* any age, already tried ChatGPT but got mediocre results. *job:* make AI *actually* save time instead of creating rework. *motivation:* productivity/time-saving + frustration relief (§2 R4; maps to quiz pain "spent just as long fixing it"). *pain:* low trust after a bad first experience; needs proven, role-specific prompts/workflows, not theory.

6. **The Role-Specialist** — *who:* accountants, lawyers, sales/marketing pros (matches Jobescape's role courses). *job:* apply AI to *my* specific function and protect my professional standing. *motivation:* job-specific effectiveness + status protection (§4 relevance/andragogy; occupation-specific adoption 12–65%). *pain:* generic AI courses don't fit their workflow, tools, or compliance constraints.

**Cross-cut:** segments 1, 3, and 4 buy primarily on **anxiety/confidence**; 2 and 6 buy on **advancement/effectiveness**; 5 buys on **frustration relief**. A single funnel can hook all six via the quiz by branching the pain-poke on age × work-status × goal — which is exactly what the 35-step quiz profiles. `[INFERENCE]`

## Candidate deep-dive topics

The 3–5 threads most worth researching deeper for this take-home:

1. **Quiz-data validation of the segments (§5).** Map the actual Jobescape 35-step quiz fields (age × gender × work-status × goal × pain-poke responses) onto the six candidate segments to see which are real, sized, and highest-value — turning inferred segments into evidence-backed ones for Part A. *(Highest priority — it's the literal Part A deliverable and needs the internal quiz/Drive data.)*

2. **Fear→confidence motivation switch across the funnel.** The purchase is driven by anxiety/FOMO but *retention* needs intrinsic/mastery/habit levers. Deep-dive the specific expectation-vs-reality gap: what the creatives+quiz *promise* (relief from fear, fast career payoff) vs what the app *delivers* (Duolingo-style drills). This is the core Part A "gap" question.

3. **Role-specific vs generic value proposition.** Andragogy + occupation-specific adoption data (12–65%) suggest role courses (Accountants/Lawyers/Sales) massively out-convert and out-retain generic "Master the Claude." Quantify demand/willingness-to-pay by role and whether the funnel routes users into the right role course.

4. **Retention mechanics benchmark (the "Duolingo for AI" claim).** Pull firmer numbers on streak/gamification retention for *adult professional* (not language) learning, plus MOOC-vs-app completion deltas, to pressure-test whether the app format actually beats the 5–15% MOOC completion baseline — feeds Part C engagement analysis too.

5. **Gender confidence gap as a growth wedge.** Women are a large, under-penetrated, confidence-gated segment converging fast on adoption. Deep-dive messaging/product accommodations (safe environment, quick wins) that could capture it — a differentiated acquisition angle.
